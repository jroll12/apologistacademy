# Demo script: full version (about 4 minutes 30 seconds)

For recording yourself on Loom or similar. The hackathon limit is 5 minutes. Spoken lines are in plain quotes; what to do on screen is in [brackets]. Read it in your own words. The short, timed script for the captioned video is in `README.md`.

**Before you record**

- Use a private window with extensions off (an unknown tracking extension slowed one test), at full screen, with no history.
- Open https://apologistacademy.pages.dev/ and do one full practice conversation first, so you know the AI is working and roughly how it answers.
- Home suggests a random question for each new visitor, so don't count on a particular one. Pick "Why does God allow suffering?" yourself from the list.
- The review takes about 10 to 15 seconds to appear. The script uses that time to talk about the safeguards.
- Turn off notifications. Close other tabs.

---

## 1. The problem (0:00 to 0:35)

[Home. Don't scroll yet.]

"Hi, I'm Jarret, and this is Apologist Academy, our entry for the AI for Church hackathon.

Most Christians want to share their faith. But when a coworker, a neighbor or one of our kids asks a hard question, like why God allows suffering, we freeze. We know the material, but we've never practiced saying it out loud to someone who pushes back.

Classes and books give us answers. They don't give us practice. That's what this is: practice a hard talk, in a low-stakes place."

## 2. Choosing a question (0:35 to 1:05)

[Scroll to More questions. Swipe or scroll the topic buttons. Point at a few icons: the cross for Jesus, the church.]

"There are sixteen hard questions in eight topics: suffering, the Bible, Jesus, science, God, right and wrong, other faiths, and the church. You can take the suggestion, pick a topic, browse them all, or hit Surprise me.

There are no accounts. Your conversations are saved only in your own browser."

[Click "Why does God allow suffering?"]

## 3. Choosing the skeptic (1:05 to 1:35)

[On the question page, click Hostile, then Balanced, then back to Curious.]

"Before I start, I choose who I'm talking to. Curious asks honest questions. Hostile pushes hard but stays civil. Balanced weighs both sides. And I can set how hard it pushes, from gentle to tough.

The skeptic is always labeled as an AI, and if you ask it, it says plainly that it's a simulated skeptic."

[Click Start. If "How it works" opens, read one line of it, then press Got it.]

## 4. The conversation (1:35 to 2:35)

[Wait for the opening line. Read it aloud.]

"The skeptic opens with the question. Now I reply, by typing or by speaking with the mic."

[Type: "That's a fair question, and I don't want to give you a cheap answer. I think part of it is that God made a world where love is real, and real love needs real choices." Send.]

"It doesn't follow a script. It responds to what I actually said, and it says so when I make a good point. If I dodge something it asked, it calls that out instead of repeating itself."

[Read its reply. Point at the five bars at the top.]

"A conversation is five turns, so there's a clear start and a clear end. And replies can be as long as I need, up to five thousand characters, so I can quote Scripture."

## 5. Coach (2:35 to 3:25)

[Click the AI button with the cross.]

"Now say I'm stuck. I tap the AI button, and Coach helps in steps. First, just a hint: one sentence that points at what the skeptic is really asking. No answer yet, because I want to find my own words first."

[Click Show outline.]

"Still stuck? An outline: a few points to cover."

[Click Show sample reply.]

"And last, a sample reply in a natural voice. I can stop at any step. I'm never forced to use any of it."

[Click Back to my reply.]

## 6. Making it personal (3:25 to 3:50)

[Type: "Can I ask if this question is personal for you?" Send.]

"A good conversation isn't a debate to win. Here I ask the skeptic if this is personal. A real person would answer, and the skeptic can too, in character."

[Read its answer. Send three short replies to finish the five turns.]

## 7. The review, and the safeguards (3:50 to 4:40)

[The review page shows "Coach is writing your review."]

"At the end, Coach writes a short review, written to me. It takes about fifteen seconds, so while it works, here's what we built in on purpose."

[Open How it works from the menu while you wait, or just keep talking.]

"The AI is always labeled. The skeptic never pretends to convert and never insults you or your faith. If someone seems to be in real distress, it stops and points to someone they can trust. Coach quotes one checked verse only and never invents books or statistics. Nothing is scored. And what you type stays in your browser."

[Back on the review.]

"Here it is: what I covered, what I could add, and one possible answer, with a verse to study from the Berean Standard Bible, checked word for word. If the AI is ever slow, the app falls back to written notes so you're never stuck."

[Open Your conversation. Point at Print or save as PDF.]

"I can open the whole conversation, or print it or save it as a PDF to bring to my small group."

## 8. Close (4:40 to 4:55)

[Go to Home.]

"Apologist Academy, built with the Apologist Project's AI platform, to help ordinary believers be ready to give an answer with gentleness and respect. Try it at the link below. Thanks for watching."

## If you run short on time

Cut section 3 and section 6. The story still holds: problem, practice, Coach, review, safeguards.

## If the AI is slow, or the review shows general notes

Say so honestly and keep going: "Sometimes the AI is slow. When it is, the app falls back to written notes for the question, so you're never stuck." Don't re-record just for that.

## If you only have one minute

Use the captioned video, `demo.mp4`. Its voiceover script is in `README.md`.
